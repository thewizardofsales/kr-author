Sharing Chanukah stories with kids: a parent’s guide

How to tell the Chanukah story to young children in simple words, plus family activities for each night, questions to ask and Chanukah books for different ages.

Chanukah is one of the most child-friendly holidays of the year. There are candles to light, dreidels to spin, latkes and doughnuts to eat, and eight nights of family time. It is also a perfect time to share stories. This guide explains how to tell the Chanukah story to young children, ideas for each night, and how to choose books for different ages.

The Chanukah story in simple words

Here is a short version you can tell young children:

Long ago, in the land of Israel, a king named Antiochus ruled over the Jewish people. He did not want them to follow their own traditions, learn Torah or celebrate Shabbat. A small group of brave Jews, called the Maccabees, stood up to the king's big army. Even though they were few, they won!

When they went back to the Holy Temple in Jerusalem, they found it messy and broken. They cleaned it and wanted to light the menorah, the special lamp in the Temple. But they found only one small jar of pure oil, enough for just one day. A miracle happened: the oil lasted for eight days! That is why we light candles for eight nights of Chanukah.

Tips for telling the story

  • Keep it short for little ones. Toddlers and preschoolers need just the main ideas: brave Maccabees, the Temple and the oil that lasted eight days.
  • Add details for older kids. Children ages seven and up can enjoy more about the Maccabees, the city of Modiin and Judah Maccabee.
  • Act it out. Use stuffed animals, puppets or toy figures to play the parts.
  • Use the menorah. Point to the candles as you count the eight days of the miracle.
  • Focus on the big ideas. Being brave, standing up for what is right, and that small lights can make a big difference.

Ideas for each night

Eight nights give you eight chances for a small family moment. Here are some ideas:

  1. Night 1: Tell the Chanukah story and light the first candle together.
  2. Night 2: Play dreidel and learn what the letters mean: nun, gimmel, hei, shin, which stand for "a great miracle happened there."
  3. Night 3: Make latkes together, and talk about why we eat foods cooked in oil.
  4. Night 4: Read a Chanukah picture book by the menorah.
  5. Night 5: Give to others. Choose a toy or food to donate.
  6. Night 6: Invite friends or neighbors to see your menorah.
  7. Night 7: Make a family craft, like a paper menorah or decorated dreidel.
  8. Night 8: Look at all eight candles together and talk about your favorite moment of the holiday.

Questions to ask after a story

  • What did the Maccabees do that was brave?
  • How do you think they felt when the oil kept burning?
  • What is something brave you have done?
  • Why do you think we put the menorah in the window?
  • How can we bring light to someone else this week?

Choosing Chanukah books by age

Ages 3 to 5

Look for short picture books with bright illustrations, simple words and familiar things like candles, dreidels and latkes. Repetition and counting books are great for this age.

Ages 5 to 8

Children enjoy stories with characters their age, gentle humor and a bit more of the history. Stories about family, sharing the holiday with neighbors and kindness work well.

Ages 7 to 12

Older children can enjoy longer stories, chapter books and graphic novels that bring the history to life in an exciting way.

A goat named Kugel comes along for the ride. Many families read it together over the eight nights.

Bring the story into your home

Chanukah stories are not only about the past. Talk about how your family celebrates and why. Share stories of Chanukahs from your own childhood or from grandparents. Children love hearing what holidays were like when their parents were little.

Keep it joyful

The most important part of sharing Chanukah with children is the feeling: warm candles, family together, and the message that even a small light can push away a lot of darkness. Whether you tell the story by heart, read a book or act it out with stuffed animals, your children will remember the time you spent together.

Chag Chanukah sameach!

Questions parents ask

How do I explain Chanukah to a toddler?

Keep it very simple: long ago, brave people called the Maccabees won a battle, and a tiny bit of oil kept the Temple menorah lit for eight days. That is why we light candles for eight nights.

What do the letters on a dreidel mean?

Outside Israel, the letters nun, gimmel, hei and shin stand for the Hebrew words for "a great miracle happened there."

What are good Chanukah activities for kids?

Lighting the menorah together, playing dreidel, making latkes, reading Chanukah books, doing crafts and giving to others are all wonderful family activities.

What Chanukah books are good for older kids?

Children ages 7 to 12 often enjoy longer stories and graphic novels that bring the history to life, such as Spin of Light: The Lichter Twins and the Miracle of Chanukah.
